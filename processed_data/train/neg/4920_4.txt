a very slick modern keeping it sensually hip revamp on the dracula story although staying with the traditional customs with quite an interesting , if not fully grasped back story of the prince of darkness . the first time i tried watching it i could only make it halfway through , before losing interest . again it gets off to a good start especially the scenes with the thieves and then their encounters with dracula , but then for me it got less involving when it hits new orleans to focus on van helsing is daughter . a great place to set it , but never took advantage of its settings despite etching a paradise in damn , where dracula could flourish . produced by wes carven and yeah they throw that name out there , but written directed by patrick lussier . artistically it had its moments with few dreamlike visuals , but some kinetic editing and cheap jolts do not help . the messy script does get considerably silly . lussier does a polished job that remains rather glassy , inserting a lot of blood the make up is suitably achieved and a lot of virgin advertising . no i do not mean virgins , it is the music company , as it does get in numerous shots and helsing is daughter works there too . oh that was not obvious planting . the soundtrack is an amusing choice of rock tunes . now the performances are all over the shop , but there are few familiar faces to spot danny masterson , sean patrick thomas , nathan fillion , shane west and lochlyn munro . gerard butler as dracula just did not come off , as not much of a presence was formed . he was simply out shined by the succulent ladies of the night ; jennifer esposito , colleen fitzpatrick and jeri ryan as dracula is brides . the likes of jonny lee millar and justine waddell are respectably okay . christopher plummer gives out a grizzled turn as van helsing and omar epps has fun with his role .
