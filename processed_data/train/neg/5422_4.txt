what noise fails to do is get us to understand its character . tim robbins plays an obsessive new yorker who cannot deal with the obtrusive noises of the city any longer , particularly car alarms . it is an odd idea for a film , which has about as much creative credibility as death wish . it is clever at points ; particularly a scene in which our hero is trying to read through hagel , i am too stupid to be understanding this . he reads and rereads a paragraph in confusion , we read it and do not get it either . just then a car alarm goes off . throughout the movie is constant interference of alarms and city noises . though , all in all it does little to help us understand our hero , who allows this all to ruin his marriage and gets distracted with side plots instead of digging deeper into his persona . the film making itself is too oblivious to notice its own sound problems , shoddy editing , and visible boom mikes . no , noise is not all bad . william hurt is at least colorful . at least the ending does not fall flat . overall it drives home a logistical point , one you have not probably thought of . at least i had not . though all in all , ninety minutes long , it could not have ended sooner . the story dragged on and seemed to be lost as soon as it started . this is another one of those movies that you might see at a film festival , but probably will not get picked up for distribution . check it out on dvd if you are really partial to someone involved in the project . otherwise skip it .
