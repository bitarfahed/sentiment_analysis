this is an extremely dense , somber , and complicated film that unravels quite slowly , revealing excruciating detail , like the attention paid in a novel , and watching this film is like watching a novel unfold . while i did not care for the narrator , as i felt he was out of balance with the rest of the performances , this film features some of the best ensemble acting i have ever seen , and the lead , summer phoenix , is fabulous . her innocence and naivete some might find implausible , sort of a cross between cinderella and alice in wonderland . i can buy that critique , but she is still fabulous , partially because she is unlike anything i have ever seen before . this film is unbelievably beautiful , filmed by eric gautier , and part of what is so unique about this film is how it does not ever show what you would expect . it is always surprising , and despite it is length , the film never reveals more than it needs to . at 163 minutes , it is extremely concise , to a fault , i would say , which is one of the wonders of this film . it is filled with brief moments which are simply stunning , some of the best you are likely to see all year , and all these moments add up in the end to an extraordinary film experience . the family moments are unique , ian holm is brilliant , and what this film has to say about the theater has not been seen in films since cassavetes opening night , or perhaps chaplin is limelight . but , believe it or not , this film is much less conventional . i never knew where this film was going , and now , having seen it , it still has multiple possibilities . this is a powerful , incredibly provocative film .
