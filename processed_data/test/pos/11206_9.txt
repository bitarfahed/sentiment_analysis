when you are watching distant you know you are not watching a french movie , there is little sex and it is mostly elliptical and people do not talk that much here , there are a few lines scattered here and there and a couple of important conversations , just to let you make sense of what is going on . it does not look american either , there are not any car chases or shoot outs or violence , unless you consider the killing of a mouse an act of blood or the daily tension of getting by a subdued catastrophe . at times , the relatively long held medium distance shots may remind you of contemplative asian cinema , but just reminds you , the director does not push things to the radical minimalism of some taiwanese filmmakers but then again , this is not a taiwanese movie , it is a turkish movie . i do not know what that means , i do not even know if that is supposed to mean something . the movie does not have a plot proper and yet , those few lines , those somewhat long held shots and that often mitigated tension gradually build a sense of something happening , a sense of plot , for lack of a better word , that grows on you . by the end of the movie you may get the feeling you are going to miss those two cousins who have many things in common but are worlds apart .
