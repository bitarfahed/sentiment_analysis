watching john cassavetes debut film is a strange experience , even if you have seen improvisational films before . the first thing you notice is it is roughness . right off , it is obvious some of the characters are screwing up their lines . but then you step back from the situation , as you sink deeper into these people is intimate exchanges and you ask yourself : do i ever stumble over my words ? the answer of course , is sure , we all do . it is unfortunate that most of the gaffes in this respect come early in the picture , because , by about twenty minutes , you have sunk so deep in you would not know it if a bomb went off behind you . the next thing you notice . . . or maybe you notice it hours or days after the film ends , is that you never saw any substantial plot , yet the themes and the poetry of the dialog and characters never leave you . in fact , the treatment of the role race plays in the everyday lives of these characters is always there , but it is so ephemeral that even they are not aware of how it is informing their opinions of themselves , their self consciousness , their perceived status , or the fate of their relationships . the title is appropriate because you get a full spectrum of blacks , whites , and grays . . . and not just in the skin pigmentation of the characters . leila goldoni truly remarkable here is an afro american caucasian , her two brothers are white and dark afro american . the irony is that they exist in what is undoubtedly the hippest most tolerant atmosphere of the time . . . beat driven upper east west manhatten . . . and there are still conflicts within and around themselves . i do not think i have ever seen a movie with such a subtle delivery or technique . it is a lot like absorbing a really great piece of gallery art and then just nodding off in bliss as you think back to the images it evoked days later . great mastering and extras on the criterion disc . arguably the first truly experimental independent film ever made .
